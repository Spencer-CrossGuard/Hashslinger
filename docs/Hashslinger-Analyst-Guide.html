Hashslinger Analyst Guide

How to run a password exposure scan and read the results. Everything here is done from the Hashslinger Running Tool. There are no settings to change.

In four steps

  1. Open Hashslinger Running Tool from the desktop (or Start menu, Hashslinger folder).
  2. Pick what to scan, enter your EDIPI and the reason or CTO number.
  3. Click Start scan and watch the progress in the same window.
  4. When it says SUCCESS, click View / edit report to read the newest report right on the server.

What this does

Hashslinger checks the passwords of Active Directory accounts against lists of known breached and weak passwords, and against common patterns such as Summer2026! or keyboard runs like 1qaz2wsx. It produces a spreadsheet of the accounts that need attention.

  • It never shows or stores anyone's password. The report lists the account and why it was flagged. It does not contain passwords or hashes.
  • It changes nothing in Active Directory. It only reads. Resetting a password or contacting the owner is a separate step that you do under your normal procedure.
  • The scan runs on the server, as a protected service account, not as you. Closing the Running Tool does not stop a scan that has started.

Start a scan

FieldWhat to enter
What to scanChoose from the list. You can only pick the scopes your administrator set up. Each includes everything underneath it.
EDIPIYour own EDIPI, exactly as issued (letters and digits, no spaces). The box shows how many characters are expected. It is checked, and an invalid or unauthorized EDIPI is refused.
Reason or CTO numberWhy you are running this scan, or the CTO / ticket number it belongs to. A few words are enough, but make them mean something to whoever reads the log later.

Click Start scan. The message "Request sent" means the server accepted it.

Everything you enter is recordedYour EDIPI, the reason, your Windows account and the time are written to a protected audit log for every scan. Use your own EDIPI only and give an honest reason.

If someone else's scan is already running, yours is kept and starts as soon as theirs finishes. You do not need to resubmit.

Watch it run

The middle of the window shows the current or last scan as a seven-step pipeline (Checks, Wordlists, Patterns, Pull from AD, Match, Report, Wipe). Finished steps turn into ticks, the current step pulses, and a coloured badge shows RUNNING, SUCCESS or FAILED. Under it are the elapsed time, how long the current step has run, how many accounts have been received, who started it, and a short log.

StateMeaning
RUNNINGIn progress. Most scans of tens of thousands of accounts take about a minute. A very large directory takes longer. The first scan after a new wordlist is added takes a few extra minutes while the lists are merged.
SUCCESSFinished. The report is ready.
FAILEDIt stopped. The window shows the reason. See Problems.

If the window says "No update for over 90 seconds", tell your administrator. The scan may have stopped.

Read the report

View / edit report opens the report in a small built-in spreadsheet, because the scan server has no Excel. You can scroll, click a column title to sort, type in Search to show only matching rows, and double-click a cell to change it. Add row and Delete selected change the rows, and Save (or Save as... to keep the original) writes the file. Copy everything puts the whole table on your clipboard so you can paste it into Excel, Outlook or Teams. Copy as CSV text is the plain file text, and Copy selected copies only the rows you clicked. Nothing changes on disk until you save.

When a scan succeeds, the Results panel summarizes it: accounts scanned, accounts flagged, how many are High and Medium (the ring chart), and the main reasons accounts were flagged (the bars). The full detail is in the report: click Open newest report (it opens in Excel or your spreadsheet program), or Open reports folder to see earlier ones. Each row is one account that needs attention. Accounts that are fine do not appear.

ColumnWhat it tells you
sAMAccountNameThe account's logon name.
OU / Distinguished NameWhere the account lives in the directory. Use it to find the owner or the team responsible.
Account TypeUser, Service (an account with a service name) or Computer.
Flag ReasonWhy it was flagged. Several reasons are separated by semicolons.
SeverityHigh or Medium (below).
Scan Run ID / DateWhich scan produced the row. Quote this when you refer to the finding.

What the flags mean

Flag reasonSeverityIn plain words
Found in HIBP breach corpusHighThe account's password appears in a list of passwords exposed in public breaches.
Found in rockyou listHighIt is on a well-known list of commonly used passwords.
Found in custom/organization term listHighIt matches a word or phrase on your organization's own list.
No password setHighThe account has no password, or is allowed to have none.
Reversible encryption enabledHighThe account is set so that its password can be recovered from the directory. That is a weaker setting.
Matched common-pattern ruleMediumThe password follows a predictable pattern (a season and year, a common word with a number, a keyboard run).
Duplicate password shared with another accountMediumSeveral accounts have the same password. This check is only on when your administrator turned it on.
LM hash presentMediumThe account still stores an old, weak form of its password.

What to do with the findings

Follow your organization's procedure for exposed or weak credentials. In general that means working the High rows first, getting the password changed for each flagged account, and passing service accounts to the team that owns them, since changing a service account's password can break the system that uses it. Re-run the scan afterwards to confirm the rows are gone.

Handle the report as sensitiveIt lists the accounts with weak or exposed passwords. Keep it in the approved location, share it only with people who need it, and do not email it or copy it to shared drives. Do not try to guess or test any account's password yourself.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
The Start scan button stays greyThe EDIPI or the reason is not complete yet. Check the EDIPI has the expected number of characters and the reason has a few words.
"Could not send the request"The request could not be saved or the scan task could not be started. Check you are in the analyst group, and tell your administrator that manual scans may be turned off.
FAILED: EDIPI is not authorized, or the wrong lengthRe-enter your EDIPI. If it is correct and still refused, your administrator may need to add it to the allowed list.
FAILED with another messageNote the message and the Run ID shown, and send both to your administrator.
"No scan has run yet"Nothing has been scanned on this server so far. Start one.
Cannot open a reportThere may be no report yet, or you may not have access to the reports folder. Ask your administrator.
The window will not openAsk your administrator whether you are in the analyst group and whether the tool has been set up on this server.

Contact: your Hashslinger administrator.

Ground rules

  • Run scans only for work you are authorized to do, with your own EDIPI and a genuine reason or CTO number.
  • Do not run scans back to back "to see what happens". Each one is logged and each one asks the domain controller for a large amount of data.
  • Treat reports as sensitive. They are not to be forwarded, posted or stored outside the approved location.
  • The tool reads only. If you think an account needs to be changed, raise it through your normal process.
  • The full administrator manual (setup, settings, upgrade, uninstall) is separate and is for administrators.
